> A standalone opinion. The comfortable story is that AI means you need to understand less. I think it is the opposite, and a central bank is the cleanest way to see why.

## Print Money, and Each Note Is Worth Less

Print money and spread it around, and every existing note buys less. That is inflation.

Now hand everyone a tool that codes. You have just printed coding ability. The result is the same — the value of baseline coding skill goes down. The thing you spent years getting good at got flooded, so it buys less. AI does not only help you; it devalues the currency you were holding.

## Inflation Hits Savers, Not Owners

But inflation does not make everyone poorer. It punishes people holding **cash** and rewards people holding **assets**.

Map that onto engineering. Rote skill — syntax, trivia, typing out a known solution — is cash, and it is being inflated away. Judgment — architecture, taste, the instinct for why something breaks under load or leaks a secret — is an asset, and its value goes *up*, because it is now the scarce thing gating a flood of cheap output.

So the real prediction is not "everyone must study harder." It is **dispersion** — the gap between shallow and deep engineers widening fast.

## Higher Knowledge, Not More

It is not more *volume*. You get to shed things — you no longer need to memorize the exact signature or the order of the flags.

What you cannot offload is *why*. Why this abstraction and not that one. Why this passes tests but fails in production. Why this is a security risk. The floor rose, and the value moved upstairs. The work is climbing to meet it.

## The Honest Crack

A tech lead earned their judgment by years spent as a junior, writing the code. If AI takes the junior rungs, where does the next generation's judgment come from? You need the experience AI is removing in order to supervise AI. I do not have a clean answer to that.

But the direction is clear. Cheap code is here. The scarce, appreciating asset is understanding — and more of us will have to go upstairs to hold it.
